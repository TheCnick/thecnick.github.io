It has taken me some weeks to settle my thoughts into an order that can be articulated. I should detail all the facts of the matter but confess myself useless to the task. And in any case, dear —, I feel uncertain of their reception. Therefore, we but do with what we are given.

I want to give amusement to my fellow man, to pry light from the shadows and bring it forth into the palms of our hands. I attempt to hover the magnifying glass over oft-overlooked details so that life seems more extraordinary than at first glance. To capture the animals—their anima (from which we get animation), if you will—so that I could bring their inner worlds to life was one of my life's goals. And through many a failed experiment, I have succeeded! I fear I have succeeded. The portrait is almost complete.


Now the hours creep in this petty place, or else they dash across the clock's face. I am unable to remember. It is as if I am in a haze; a fog has filled the room and my head with it as well. I have but vague impressions of what I am supposed to do, of my work, of myself.


and I feel a presence—or perhaps, more accurately, presences—of persons unknown. At times it seems they follow me, watching. At other times, I scream into the void without a voice, with no mouth, and there's nothing. They are all gone. Disappeared to I know not where.

It went on rotating (back and forth!) in this way for some weeks, when the— It happened. I had been working late that night, as is my usual custom, when the portrait I was working on cracked open. The paint surface split down the middle! That soft and pliable smile, the smile I had spent many an hour on, was now changed to a half-fiendish grin!


The days now bleed into each other. Memories once so vivid and fresh, like an impressionist's flowers, are now dull and bleak like a seashore by Turner.



(The beast is upon my back, and I'm being ridden to the grave.)


Its very eyes seem now malevolent, and with each passing day I can't make myself touch my hand nor my brush to its congealed surface. Its ire grows. Its condemning gaze follows me about the room. It's as if, and I burn to confess it/I shudder to confess it—it's as if all the presences have combined into this! this entity within my painting.
